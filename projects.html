Career Landscape in Data Science & ML

Conducted an exploratory data analysis on 20,000+ global Kaggle survey responses to uncover industry hiring requirements, compensation drivers, and technical tooling trends.

A comprehensive career analytics study using Kaggle's community survey dataset (171 countries/territories) to evaluate educational qualifications, compensation benchmarks, task distributions across job roles, and core data science workflows.

  • Problem: assess how educational degrees, technical experience (coding vs. ML), and global market demand influence role responsibilities and earning potential.
  • Methods: categorical survey data cleaning, multiple-response frequency aggregation, contingency table analysis, cross-regional filtering, and multivariate visualization using R (tidyverse, dplyr, ggplot2, tidyr).
  • Outcome: identified master's degrees as dominant in analytics/business roles (~50%) and PhDs in research (58%); found coding experience positively correlates with salary while ML experience showed negligible standalone correlation; mapped Python (~40%), SQL (~20%), and R (~15%) as top languages alongside linear/logistic regression (25%) and tree ensembles (35%).

Machine Learning · Environmental Data Science

Predictive Modeling of Daily Snowfall

Trained Random Forest and XGBoost regression models on 49 years of Sierra Nevada climate data to predict daily snowfall accumulation from temperature and humidity patterns[cite: 6].

A data science study analyzing 11,444 winter-season daily records (1970–2019) from UC Berkeley's Central Sierra Snow Lab and NOAA weather stations to model non-linear precipitation dynamics for California water management[cite: 6].

  • Problem: accurately estimate total daily snowfall depth (cm) in the Sierra Nevada during snow-prone months (October through May) using surface temperature and humidity features[cite: 6].
  • Methods: multi-source dataset merging, seasonal filtering, zero-inflation analysis, EDA, Random Forest Regression, and XGBoost modeling (Python, pandas, scikit-learn, XGBoost, seaborn)[cite: 6].
  • Outcome: XGBoost achieved an RMSE of 7.42 cm and an R² of 0.490, highlighting maximum temperature and relative humidity as primary predictors while identifying the challenges of extreme tail-event underprediction[cite: 6].
